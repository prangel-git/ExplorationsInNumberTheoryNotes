### Lemma

If $m \mid n$ and $x \equiv a \mod n$, then $x \equiv a \mod n$

## Theorem

The Cartesian product of rings is a ring where the operations are defined pointwise based on the operations of the rings.

# Sunzi's remider theorem

Let $m$ and $n$ be relatively prime. Then the rings $\mathbb{Z}/(mn)$ and $\mathbb{Z}/(m) \times \mathbb{Z}/(n)$ are isomorphic.

In practice, for every $a \in \mathbb{Z}/(n)$ and for every $b \in \mathbb{Z}/(m)$ there exist an $x \in \mathbb{Z}/(nm)$ such that $x \equiv a \mod n$ and $x \equiv b \mod m$.

In other words, when $n$ and $m$ are relatively prime, there is an $x \in \mathbb{Z}/(nm)$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x \equiv a & \mod n \\
x \equiv b & \mod m
\end{aligned}
$$

## Corollary

If $\gcd(n, m) = 1$, then $a \in \mathbb{Z}/(nm)$ is a unit, if and only if $a$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Z}/(n)$ and $\mathbb{Z}/(m)$. Therefore, Sunzi's reminder's theorem also proves that $\phi(nm)=\phi(n) \phi(m)$. That is, the totient function is multiplicative.
